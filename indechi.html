Lesson 01 · h1–h6

One title, then the rest.

A heading says what the next stretch of page is about. The levels nest: h1 for the page, h2 for a section, h3 for a part of that section.

What the levels are for

This page has one h1. The line you just passed is an h2. Skip a level and a screen reader loses the map, the way a book would if chapter 4 arrived with no chapter 3.

A heading is a label, not a size

CSS decides how large it looks. The tag decides where it sits in the outline. Change the look in the stylesheet. Keep the level honest in the HTML.

Pictures with a job

An img needs an alt that describes the picture for someone who cannot see it. A figure groups the picture with its figcaption.

Alt text

Write what is in the picture. “Owl” is thin. “A great grey owl facing the camera” is enough for this page.

Captions

The caption is extra, for everyone. It can hold the credit. The alt still has to stand alone, because some people never see the caption.

The lower levels

Most pages never need to go past h3. When a section really does get deep, the next tags are waiting.

h4 marks a point inside an h3

Use it when the h3 has parts of its own.

h5 is rarer

If you reach h5 often, the page may be trying to be an outline of the whole subject. Split it.

h6 is the last level

There is no h7. This one is styled small and in capitals so the end of the scale is visible.

<figure>
  <img src="images/owl.jpg" alt="A great grey owl facing the camera.">
  <figcaption>Christoph Nolte · Unsplash</figcaption>
</figure>
